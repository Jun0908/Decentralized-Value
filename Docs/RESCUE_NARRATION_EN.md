## 00:00–00:20 — Introduction

Who gets to decide what better means?

Most systems hide that decision inside a weighted score. Value Decentralization keeps values independent: measure once, preserve the tradeoffs, and let different pools support different definitions of progress.

## 00:20–00:43 — Hire

Rescue Room is an incident-response game for a fictional Ethereum protocol. The Commander cannot see the true cause. It hired a specialist AI, received an analysis, and paid five test tokens through a real Sepolia escrow transaction. The diagnosis itself is not proven correct.

## 00:43–01:03 — Decide

We returned that purchased analysis to the Commander. In three real follow-up calls, it waited twice, then paused withdrawals. These protocol actions are simulated. This short demonstration stops after three decisions and advances the simulator to the end.

## 01:03–01:24 — Compare

The result: no user loss, about seventy-one percent of demand served, and five game credits spent. But doing nothing served all demand, with no loss or spending. This time, the AI lost. Paying for intelligence and getting value are different questions.

## 01:24–01:47 — Allocate

Now the same evidence goes to three independent pools. User protection, protocol availability, and treasury stewardship each use their own axis. These are allocation previews, not paid rewards. We never collapse them into one overall score.
